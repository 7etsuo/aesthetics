For Grok Imagine i'm trying to make a dictionary of the smallest controllable properties that combine to create an image’s overall “timbre.”. E.g., The basis vectors of visual appearance.

The clean technical umbrella is:

## **image-formation parameters**

A useful informal term is:

## **visual timbre dimensions**

Instead of testing compound labels like “1980s fantasy,” “cinematic,” or “VHS,” test these atomic dimensions one at a time.

## 1. Tonal response

* **black level**: lifted blacks vs deep blacks
* **shadow density**: open shadows vs murky shadows
* **shadow roll-off / toe**: gradual vs abrupt descent into black

* **dynamic range**: compressed vs expansive

* **local contrast**
* **microcontrast**
* **gamma response**
* **exposure bias**

* **overexposure character**

## The important separation

There are at least five distinct layers people casually collapse into the word “look”:

1. **What was physically placed before the camera**
2. **How it was lit**
3. **What lens recorded it**
4. **What medium recorded it**
5. **How it was processed and reproduced**

Your old fantasy reference is a combination of all five. That is why phrases like “film emulation” failed. They modified only the final layer.

## How to test this properly

Use the same image, composition, seed, and prompt. Change exactly one parameter at a time.

For each dimension, test three strengths:

* none
* moderate
* extreme

For example:

> Preserve everything exactly. Change only the microcontrast. Version A: high microcontrast.

Then:

> Preserve everything exactly. Change only the microcontrast. Version B: low microcontrast.

Do this for:

* microcontrast
* edge softness
* optical diffusion
* highlight roll-off
* black level
* color separation
* chroma density
* halation
* lens flare
* grain structure
* transfer softness
* material roughness
* lighting contrast

That gives you an actual **visual-timbre atlas** rather than a pile of vague aesthetic names.

The most accurate one-sentence description of what you are building is:

> **A controlled taxonomy of image-formation dimensions, tested independently to discover the visual basis vectors that produce recognizable media aesthetics.**
